### Our first recommendation (we'll confirm it after this questionnaire)

- **Site type:** a multi-page personal brand site with the blog at its centre.
- **Sitemap:** `Home` · `Blog` · `Projects` · `About` · `Contact`, plus optional `Now` and `Uses` pages, which are popular on developer sites.
- **Build:** a fast static site (e.g. Astro) with Markdown blog posts, an RSS feed, SEO and auto-generated social-share images.
- **Hosting:** free on **GitHub Pages**.
- **Domain:** a custom domain such as `yourname.dev` is strongly recommended for a personal brand. It usually costs about ₹800–1,800 (≈ $10–20) a year.

## 2. Your story: interview prompts 🎙️

*This becomes your About page and sets the voice for the whole site. Answer as if you're chatting with a friend over coffee.*

**2.1 ★ Your journey in 4–6 bullet points.** How did you get into tech? What were the turning points? Where are you now, and where are you heading?

**2.2 ★ What do people come to you for?** What do friends, classmates or colleagues ask you to help with?

**2.3 What are you excited about or learning right now?**

**2.4 A tech opinion you hold strongly**, even if not everyone agrees with it. A strong point of view makes a personal brand memorable.

**2.5 Something you're proud of.** A project, a win, or a hard problem you solved. What was the situation, what did you do, and what happened as a result?

**2.6 Life outside tech.** Hobbies, interests, a fun fact. These make your site feel human.

## 5. Projects & credibility 🛠️

**5.1 ★ Projects to feature (1–6).** For each one: name · what it does (one line) · your role · tech used · links (GitHub / live demo) · the result or what you learned.
> **No projects yet?** No problem. We'll launch with a "What I'm building" section that grows with you. Your GitHub account is brand new, so we'll plan for that growth from the start.

**5.2 Experience highlights.** Current and past roles, companies and key achievements. Numbers help, e.g. "cut page load time by 40%". This is optional for a personal brand, but it builds trust.

**5.3 Education & certifications.** Degree, institution and year, plus any certifications (AWS, Google Cloud, Microsoft, etc.).

**5.4 Proof & social signals.** Talks, hackathons, awards, publications, open-source contributions, press mentions, and testimonials (with the person's name, role and permission).

**5.5 Skills & tools.** The languages, frameworks and tools you use most. If you'd like a `Uses` page, add your setup too: laptop, editor, terminal and favourite apps.

## 6. Look & feel: bold & modern 🎨

**6.1 ★ Choose a creative direction** (or mix two). To compare all four visually, open the style-directions preview (`website-brief/style-directions/index.html`).
- ☐ **A: Neon Night.** A dark background, an electric lime accent, monospace details and a soft glow. Unmistakably "developer".
- ☐ **B: Gradient Pop.** Bright and airy, with vivid violet → pink → orange gradients and big rounded headlines. Energetic and friendly.
- ☐ **C: Brutalist Bold.** Stark black and white plus one loud colour, chunky borders and oversized type. Edgy and memorable.
- ☐ **D: Editorial Tech.** A magazine-style layout with giant serif headlines, a strong grid and one accent colour. Ideal for a brand built on writing.

**6.2 Websites you like** (2–3, any industry) and what you like about each. Add one you dislike, and say why.
> Not sure where to start? Look at a few well-known developer sites and tell us what you think: joshwcomeau.com (playful and interactive) · brittanychiang.com (sleek, dark portfolio) · kentcdodds.com (blog-first personal brand) · leerob.com (ultra-minimal). Designs change over time, so just react to whatever you see.

**6.3 Colours.** Any favourites, or colours to avoid? Accent shortlist: ☐ Electric blue ☐ Violet ☐ Lime / neon green ☐ Orange / coral ☐ Hot pink ☐ Surprise me

**6.4 Light or dark?** ☐ Dark ☐ Light ☐ Both, with a toggle (recommended)

**6.5 Motion:** ☐ Minimal ☐ Subtle: scroll reveals and hover effects (recommended) ☐ Expressive: an animated hero and playful interactions

**6.6 Photo of you:** ☐ I'll send a photo ☐ Turn my photo into a stylised illustration that matches the site ☐ No photo; use a monogram or an illustrated avatar
> Tip: a phone photo taken facing a window, against a plain background and framed from the shoulders up works well.

**6.7 Logo:** ☐ I have one (please attach it) ☐ Design a simple monogram from my initials ☐ Text only (just my name)
